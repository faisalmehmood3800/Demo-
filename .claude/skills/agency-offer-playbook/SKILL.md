# Agency offer playbook

Sources: the offers lecture in the user's chosen tutorials (notes in `../pro-website-playbook/references/tutorial-notes-2026-11-27.md`, section D), the LinkedIn sales and selling-psychology tutorials (`../pro-website-playbook/references/tutorial-notes-batch2-2026-11-27.md`, sections G and M) and the pricing research done in this workspace. Use it with the `sales` skills for research, outreach and proposals, and with unslop's copy rules.

## 1. What an offer is

An offer makes the client feel they get far more than they pay. It is not a percentage discount. Examples: Domino's "30 minutes or it's free"; Zappos "not sure of your size? we send both, keep one, return one free"; Easypaisa "send money instantly anywhere in Pakistan".

Sell the benefit the buyer is actually paying for, and name the problem it removes: a power bank is "charge your phone two or three times and stop hunting for sockets", not "10,000 mAh"; an office chair is "ten hours a day without back pain", not "adjustable, good plastic". For the agency: "your shop answers every WhatsApp within a minute, even at night", not "n8n chatbot".

The tutorials call marketing "manipulation" and show a countdown timer that resets on every visit. The agency doesn't do either: persuasion here means making a true offer easy to understand and easy to say yes to.

## 2. Five components (check every offer against all five)

1. Believable. Proof beats promises: live demos, real past work, real reviews. A risk reversal must be believable; "I'll work free forever" destroys trust. Label demos as demos.
2. High perceived value. Sell the outcome, not the feature: "patients book on WhatsApp at 2 am while you sleep", not "n8n chatbot with RAG". Bundle related work (site plus WhatsApp bot plus Google Business Profile) and add low-cost bonuses that matter to the client (a month of free edits, a short training video).
3. Low risk for the client. Take the risk yourself: staged payments, a satisfaction clause, a trial period, a clear refund rule. Show guarantees plainly, not in small print.
4. Real urgency. A genuine reason to act now: limited slots this month, a launch-week price, a seasonal deadline (Ramadan, Eid, wedding season). Never fake it: no countdown timers that reset, no invented "regular" prices, no false scarcity. That is deceptive and can break consumer-protection rules.
5. Simple. Understood in ten seconds, in plain words, ideally one line.

## 3. Service ladder (Pakistan, PKR)

Market context from research (GoDaddy Pakistan guide and local agencies, 2026): basic business sites Rs 25,000 to 60,000; professional custom sites Rs 75,000 to 250,000; advanced sites Rs 100,000 to 350,000; hosting Rs 5,000 to 10,000 a year.

| Package | Contents | Starting price |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | One-page site, WhatsApp button, mobile-ready, basic SEO | Rs 25,000 |
| Business | Up to five pages, custom design to the unslop standard, motion, SEO basics, Google Business Profile setup | Rs 40,000 |
| Premium | Business plus a scroll-animated hero and a WhatsApp or website chatbot | Rs 70,000+ |
| Care plan | Hosting, updates, small edits, bot document updates | Rs 3,000 to 5,000 a month |

- First three to five clients: launch pricing in exchange for a review and permission to show the work; then raise to market rate.
- Terms on every job: 50% advance, written scope, two revision rounds included, extra work quoted separately, domain and hosting in the client's name.
- Foreign clients: price in USD for value, not by converting PKR.

## 4. Offer templates (fill from the client's business; no invented numbers)

- "A website your [patients/customers] can book from in two taps, live in [N] days. If you don't like the first design, you don't pay the advance balance."
- "Your shop answers WhatsApp at 2 am: prices, timings and orders, from your own price list. First month of updates free."
- Bundle: "Website + WhatsApp assistant + Google Maps listing, one price, one point of contact."
- Add-on pricing makes bundles easy to accept: the main service at its price, and the related piece for a small extra (tutorial example: copywriting $400, landing page +$50; agency example: website, plus a WhatsApp FAQ bot for a small add-on in the first month). Bonuses should be cheap for you and useful to them: a short training video, a month of edits, a launch-day check.

## 5. Getting clients

### LinkedIn sales system (from the tutorial, adapted)

1. Right prospect: only businesses that need the service and can pay (recently funded, hiring, posting about the problem); connect with the person who decides, not junior staff. Write the ideal customer profile down (industry, size, country, title, pain).
2. Profile that proves it: clear professional photo; headline = who you help + what result + time frame, e.g. "I help clinics answer every WhatsApp enquiry within a minute"; a simple banner with real proof (client logos or numbers only with permission and only if true); About section that tells your process as a short story; projects and demos listed, demos labelled as demos.
3. Content that brings leads, not likes: a weekly plan (for example one teaching post, one build or case-study post, one client result), problem-agitate-solution structure, real numbers and stories, and a clear call to action on roughly one post in five.
4. Attract, don't chase: thoughtful comments on ideal clients' posts (summarise, add a point they missed, give your angle); after a few, many will connect or message. In DMs ask about their business and give something useful before mentioning price; bigger deals take weeks.
5. Light automation: AI to summarise long posts, draft comments and post ideas that you then edit; a CRM or sheet for every lead's stage. Keep outreach personal and low-volume: bulk scraping or auto-messaging breaks LinkedIn's terms and gets accounts restricted.

### Portfolio before niche

Build many small labelled demos across industries while learning (the tutorial suggests aiming for dozens of agents), then pick one niche and show only the relevant ones; a niche makes clients easier to find and justifies higher prices.

### Other channels

- Channels that work: showing the work as content, personalised LinkedIn or WhatsApp outreach to a specific business with a specific observation, and Upwork or Fiverr proposals written for that job. Generic mass messages do not work.
- Lead pipeline: define the ideal customer profile (industry, size, platform, city, decision-maker title), keep leads in a sheet or small CRM with stages (new, contacted, replied, meeting, client, not interested) and notes. The `sales` skills do the research and outreach drafts.
- Practise the pitch: record voice notes selling the offer, and refine until it is clear in one breath.
- Personal brand without showing a face: real name, consistent logo, posts of real work, before-and-after reels, case studies with permission.
- Never claim a team size, client count, award or result that is not true, and get permission before showing a client's work.
